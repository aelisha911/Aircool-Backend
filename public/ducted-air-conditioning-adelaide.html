The basics

What is ducted air conditioning?

Ducted air conditioning is a whole-home system with one outdoor unit and one indoor fan coil hidden in the roof space. Insulated ducts carry heated or cooled air to ceiling outlets in each room, and a return air grille draws air back. A reverse cycle ducted system heats in winter and cools in summer from the same outlets.

Most Adelaide homes that choose ducted do it for three reasons: every room is covered, the wall stays clear of indoor units, and one controller runs the whole house. With zoning, you still pick which rooms get conditioned and when.

Inside the roof

How a ducted system works

  • 1Indoor fan coil. Sits in the roof space, blows air across a coil that heats or cools it.
  • 2Outdoor unit. Holds the compressor and moves heat between the outside air and the fan coil through insulated copper pipes.
  • 3Insulated ductwork. Carries air to each room. Your Home recommends ducts insulated to at least R1.5 with sealed joints.
  • 4Zone dampers. Motorised gates that open or close each zone from the wall controller or an app.
  • 5Ceiling outlets. Discreet grilles that spread air through each room.
  • 6Return air grille. Draws air back to the fan coil through a filter. Undersize it and the whole system struggles.
Before we quote

Sizing and zoning your ducted reverse cycle system

An oversized system cycles on and off and wastes power. An undersized one runs flat out on a 40°C afternoon and still can't hold the house. Your Home makes the same point: a correctly sized unit uses less energy. So we measure before we price anything.

  • Floor area and ceiling height of every room you want conditioned
  • Ceiling and wall insulation, window size and which way the windows face
  • Roof space height and access for the fan coil and duct runs
  • Where the outdoor unit can sit without bothering you or the neighbours
  • Single-phase or three-phase power, and switchboard capacity
  • How your household uses the house: day zones, night zones, rooms rarely used

What is zoning in a ducted system?

Zoning splits a ducted system into areas, such as living rooms and bedrooms, with motorised dampers that open or close each zone from the controller. You condition only the rooms in use, so the system does less work. Your Home notes zone systems let living and sleeping areas run at different settings.

A typical setup runs living areas through the day and bedrooms at night. Zoning works best when the system is designed for it from the start, so the fan coil can handle the airflow change when zones close.

Rebates in South Australia

South Australia's Retailer Energy Productivity Scheme (REPS) includes an activity for installing an efficient new ducted reverse cycle air conditioner (activity HC2B). REPS discounts are delivered through energy retailers and their accredited providers, so eligibility and the amount depend on your household and the offer at the time. Ask your energy retailer, and ask us to quote a model that meets the scheme's efficiency rules.

What are the disadvantages of ducted air conditioning?

A higher upfront cost than split systems, the need for usable roof space, and energy lost through the ductwork. energy.gov.au notes ducted systems are less efficient than wall-mounted units for that reason. Good duct design, ducts insulated to at least R1.5 with sealed joints, and zoning keep those losses down.

The honest trade-offs

Downsides worth knowing before you buy

  • Upfront cost. Ducted is the most expensive way to air condition a home. You pay for whole-home coverage and a clean look.
  • Duct losses. Air travels through a hot or cold roof before it reaches the room. Poorly sealed or crushed ducts waste the energy you paid for.
  • One mode for the house. Every zone heats or every zone cools. You can't cool one room while heating another.
  • Roof space. Some older Adelaide homes with low-pitched roofs have little room for a fan coil and duct runs. We check this before quoting.
  • Return air design. An undersized return grille makes the system noisy and weak. Your Home warns that without a proper return air path, the system pulls in air from where it shouldn't.
Servicing

Ducted air conditioning service in Adelaide

How often should a ducted air conditioner be serviced?

Once a year suits most Adelaide homes, ideally in spring before summer demand. Systems that run hard all year, or homes with pets, dust or nearby building work, benefit from more frequent return air filter cleaning between services. Book early: the first heatwave fills every technician's diary.

What a ducted service covers

  • Return air filter cleaned or replaced
  • Fan coil, drain tray and condensate line checked and cleared
  • Outdoor coil inspected and cleared of leaves and debris
  • Refrigerant pressures and supply air temperature measured
  • Every zone damper and the wall controller tested
  • Electrical connections checked and tightened
  • Duct connections in the roof checked for leaks or crushing

Servicing on a schedule? See our service and maintenance plans.

Signs your ducted system needs a repair

  • Some rooms never reach the set temperature
  • A zone won't open or close from the controller
  • Weak airflow from outlets that used to blow hard
  • A water stain on the ceiling under the fan coil
  • Ice on the copper pipes or the outdoor unit
  • An error code on the wall controller
  • Banging, rattling or humming from the roof space

See water or ice? Switch the system off and call 1300 019 568. Running it can make the damage worse.

Repair or replace

When replacing a ducted system makes more sense

You may have heard of the $5,000 rule. It's an American rule of thumb: multiply the system's age in years by the repair quote, and if the answer is over 5,000, look at replacing.

It's a reasonable prompt, but it ignores things that matter here. We look at these instead, and tell you plainly which way we'd go.

  • Refrigerant. Older systems that run on R22 face a refrigerant being phased out in Australia, which makes regassing harder.
  • Warranty. Daikin and Mitsubishi Electric both offer a five-year parts and labour warranty on domestic ducted systems.
  • Comfort. If the system can't hold the house on a 40°C day, a repair won't change that.
  • Running cost. Compare the Zoned Energy Rating Label of a new model for Adelaide's climate zone against what you run now.

Ducted air conditioning FAQs

Which ducted air conditioner is the best in Australia?

No single brand is best for every home. Daikin and Mitsubishi Electric both back domestic ducted systems with a five-year parts and labour warranty in Australia. The right choice depends on the capacity your home needs, the zoning controller, noise levels and the Zoned Energy Rating Label figures for Adelaide's climate zone. We'll recommend a model and explain why.

What is the 3 minute rule for aircon?

After an air conditioner switches off, wait about three minutes before restarting it. The pause lets refrigerant pressures settle so the compressor doesn't start under load. Many systems build in a three-minute restart delay, so a short wait before the outdoor unit starts is normal, not a fault.

What is the $5,000 rule for AC?

It's an American rule of thumb: multiply the system's age in years by the repair quote, and if the answer is over 5,000, consider replacing it. It ignores Australian factors such as the R22 refrigerant phase-out, warranty, rebates and running costs, so treat it as a prompt for a conversation, not a verdict.

Can ducted reverse cycle replace my ducted gas heating?

Yes. A reverse cycle system can take over the heating and add cooling. Before quoting, we check system capacity, duct condition, the return air path and your electrical supply, because the Your Home guide notes ducts need to be larger if they are also used for cooling. If you would rather keep the gas heater, we service and repair those too.

Can I reuse my evaporative cooler ducts for reverse cycle?

Usually not as they are. Evaporative ductwork is designed for a different job, and Your Home says ducts carrying refrigerated air must be insulated to at least R1.5 with sealed joints. We inspect the existing ducts and tell you what can stay.

Does ducted reverse cycle heat well enough for an Adelaide winter?

A correctly sized system does. Adelaide's long-term average winter minimum is about 8°C, and reverse cycle systems keep extracting heat from outside air at those temperatures. Sizing for your coldest rooms is the part that matters.

Do you service ducted systems you didn't install?

Yes. We service and repair ducted systems from the major brands, whoever installed them.
