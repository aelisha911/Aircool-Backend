The basics

What is ducted gas heating?

Ducted gas heating warms a whole house from one gas heater, usually sitting in the roof space or outside against a wall. A fan blows air across a heat exchanger, and insulated ducts carry that warm air to vents in each room. A return air grille draws cooled air back to the heater to be warmed again.

It is a heating-only system. There is no cooling mode, which is the main thing that separates it from a ducted reverse cycle air conditioner. Many Adelaide homes run gas ducted heating through winter and a separate evaporative cooler or split system through summer.

Gas ducted heating has been installed in South Australian homes for decades, so a large number of systems around Adelaide are now well past their first service life. A good proportion of our heating work is not new installs at all. It is getting an existing system running properly again, and telling the owner honestly whether it is worth keeping.

Gas ducted heating installation and replacement

Most of the heating installs we quote in Adelaide fall into one of three situations: a first-time ducted system in a home that has never had one, a straight replacement of a heater that has failed, or a changeover where the owner wants to move to or from reverse cycle.

What we look at before quoting

Replacing an old heater is not always a like-for-like swap. Duct sizing and return air requirements have changed over the years, and a system that was adequate when the house was built may not suit the home now, especially after an extension. We check that before quoting rather than after.

Can I keep my existing ducts when replacing a gas heater?

Often, yes, if they are in sound condition and the right size for the new unit. Ducts crush, split and come loose over time, and an old return air path can be undersized for a modern heater. We inspect the ductwork before quoting and tell you plainly what can stay and what should be replaced.

Thinking about reverse cycle instead? A ducted reverse cycle system heats and cools from the same outlets, which suits households that want one system for the whole year. We quote both, and we will tell you which we would choose for your home and why. See ducted air conditioning.
Repairs

Gas ducted heating repairs and common fault symptoms

Why is my ducted gas heating not heating?

The causes range from simple to serious: a thermostat or controller fault, a blocked return air filter, a pilot or ignition problem, a gas supply issue, or a fault in the heater itself. The symptom alone does not identify the cause, which is why a ducted gas heater needs to be inspected on site rather than diagnosed over the phone.

Below are the problems Adelaide homeowners describe most often when they call us about a gas heater. We are not going to guess which one applies to your system from a description, but recognising the symptom helps you explain it when you book.

What you might notice

  • No heat at all. The system runs but blows cool or room-temperature air, or will not start.
  • Uneven heating. Some rooms warm up and others never do, or the far end of the house stays cold.
  • The system cuts out. It runs for a while, then shuts down by itself and may restart later.
  • Unusual noises. Banging, rattling, squealing or a rumble from the roof space or the unit.
  • Weak airflow. Vents that used to push air firmly now barely move it.
  • Controller faults. A blank screen, an error code, or a thermostat the system ignores.
  • A smell when it runs. A burning or dusty smell on first use each winter is common; a persistent or sharp smell is not.

What happens when we come out

  1. We inspect the heater, the ductwork, the return air path and the controller.
  2. We identify the fault and explain what we have found in plain terms.
  3. You get a fixed written quote for the repair before any work starts.
  4. If the system is not worth repairing, we say so and show you why.

Servicing

Ducted heating servicing and maintenance

How often should ducted gas heating be serviced?

Most manufacturers and gas safety bodies recommend having a ducted gas heater inspected by a qualified person at regular intervals, and your heater's manual will state what applies to your model. Book it before winter rather than during it. Heaters that have sat unused since spring are the ones that fail on the first genuinely cold night.

A gas heater service is a different job from an air conditioner service, and it matters more. A gas appliance burns fuel inside your home and vents the products of that combustion. When a heat exchanger cracks or a flue is obstructed, the warning signs are not always obvious to the person living there, which is the reason the inspection has to be done by someone qualified to do it.

What a service generally involves

The exact checks depend on the make and model, and on what the manufacturer specifies for your heater. In general terms a service covers the condition and operation of the heater, the burner and ignition, the filters and airflow, the ductwork and vents that are accessible, and the controller.

Why we do not publish a fixed checklist. Service requirements differ between brands and models, and a generic tick-list published on a website is not the same as what your heater's manufacturer actually requires. We work to what applies to your system and tell you what we found.

Want servicing on a schedule across all your systems? See service and maintenance.

Does a gas heater need servicing if it seems to be working?

Yes. A gas heater can run and still produce heat while a fault is developing inside it, and some of those faults have safety consequences rather than comfort ones. A heater that seems fine is the normal condition at the time of a service, not a reason to skip one.

If your heater has not been looked at in several years, or you have just moved into a house and have no history for the system, that is worth booking before you rely on it through a winter.

Inside the system

How ducted gas heating works, and when it suits a home

  • 1The heater. Gas burns inside a sealed heat exchanger. A fan blows household air across the outside of it, so the air is warmed without touching the flame or its exhaust.
  • 2Ductwork. Insulated ducts carry warm air from the heater across the roof space to each room.
  • 3Vents. Ceiling or floor outlets release warm air into the room. Zoned systems can shut off the vents in rooms you are not using.
  • 4Return air grille. A large grille, usually in a hallway, draws air back to the heater to be warmed again. If it is blocked or undersized, the whole system struggles.
  • 5Flue. Carries the products of combustion outside. It is the part of a gas heater with no equivalent in a reverse cycle system, and the reason gas heaters need qualified inspection.

Ducted gas heating tends to suit homes that already have a gas connection and usable roof space, households that want the whole house warm rather than one room, and owners who already have evaporative cooling and want heating to match the same kind of ducted layout.

It suits you less well if the house has no gas connection, if roof space is tight, or if you would rather run one system for both heating and cooling. In that last case reverse cycle is usually the better fit, and we will say so.

Not sure what you have

Is it ducted gas heating or ducted reverse cycle?

How do I know if I have gas ducted heating or reverse cycle?

Look for an outdoor unit. A ducted reverse cycle system has a large fan unit outside the house; ducted gas heating does not. A gas system has a flue venting through the roof or wall, a gas supply line, and it heats only. If the same vents also blow cold air in summer, it is reverse cycle.

This comes up more often than you would expect, especially after buying a house. The quickest checks:

Questions

Ducted gas heating FAQs

Do you repair ducted gas heaters, or only install new ones?

We do both, plus servicing. Repairs and servicing make up a large share of our heating work, including on systems we did not install and on brands other companies have walked away from.

My gas heater is blowing cold air. What is wrong with it?

There are several possible causes, from a controller or thermostat fault to an ignition problem, a blocked filter or a fault in the heater itself. The symptom does not identify the cause on its own, so we inspect it on site rather than guess. Turn the system off and call 1300 019 568.

Some rooms heat up and others stay cold. Is that a fault?

It can be. Uneven heating is sometimes a duct problem, such as a duct that has come loose, been crushed or lost insulation in the roof space. It can also be an undersized or blocked return air path, a zoning fault, or a heater that is too small for the house. All of those are things we check on site.

How long does a ducted gas heater last?

It varies a great deal with the model, how hard it has worked and whether it has been serviced. Rather than quote a number, we would look at the condition of your particular system and tell you whether it is worth continuing to repair. A system that needs repeated repairs, or where parts are difficult to get, is usually telling you something.

Should I repair my gas heater or replace it?

It depends on the fault, the age and condition of the unit, and whether parts are still available for it. An isolated fault on an otherwise sound heater is usually worth repairing. Repeated breakdowns, or a fault in a major component on an old system, usually point the other way. We inspect it and tell you plainly which we would choose and why.

Can ducted reverse cycle replace my gas ducted heating?

Yes, and it adds cooling. Before quoting we check the capacity needed, the condition of the ductwork, the return air path and the electrical supply, because ducts used for cooling have different requirements from ducts carrying only warm air. If you would rather keep the gas heater, we service and repair those too.

Can I use my existing gas heating ducts for a reverse cycle system?

Sometimes, but not automatically. Ductwork carrying refrigerated air has different insulation and sealing requirements, and the duct sizing may not suit. We inspect the existing ducts and tell you what can stay rather than assuming.

Do you work on gas heaters from any brand?

Yes. We work on all the major ducted gas heating brands found in Adelaide homes, including Braemar, Brivis, Bonaire and Rinnai, whoever installed them.
